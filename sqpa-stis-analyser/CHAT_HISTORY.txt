Good question — the short answer is the data IS correct, and the difference is expected. Here's why:

**How Amazon reports it**

In the STIS report, `Search Term Impression Share` is recorded **per campaign per day**, not per keyword. Amazon measures what share of the total eligible pool *that specific campaign* captured on *that specific date*.

**Why each campaign shows a different IS**

The report covers a date range. Each campaign was likely active on a **different set of days** within that range:

| Campaign | Active days | Total Impressions | Eligible Pool | IS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Auto/New | fewer days (newer campaign) | 16 | 191 | 8.39% |
| Generic/Phrase | more days / higher bids | 2,583 | 19,457 | 13.28% |

The eligible impressions column (`Impressions / IS`) represents **"across the days this campaign served, how large was the total auction pool?"** — so a campaign active for 4 weeks naturally shows a much larger eligible pool than one active for 1 week, even if the daily IS is similar.

**What our code does**

For each campaign group we compute a **weighted-average IS** (weighted by impressions across dates) and then:

```
Eligible Impressions = Σ Impressions / (weighted_avg_IS / 100)
```

So the variation you see is real — it reflects each campaign's coverage window within the report period, not a calculation bug.

**If you want keyword-level IS** (one single number regardless of campaign), that's already shown in the main keyword table (`IS Share` there uses the impression-weighted average across *all* campaigns combined). The bottom table intentionally shows the per-campaign breakdown so you can see which campaigns are winning more of the auction.
